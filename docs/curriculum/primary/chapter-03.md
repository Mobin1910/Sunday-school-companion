## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A crowded street market in bright sun — fruit stalls, bunting, a stage far off with musicians on it. A small boy in a blue T-shirt and backpack stands hemmed in by grown-ups, one hand on a man's back, craning up. Halo glows beside his shoulder. The boy says, “I can’t see!” A chalkboard behind him reads “Good Food Brighter Days”.
2. The same market. The boy has turned round, chin resting on his fist, thinking. Halo floats by the mango baskets and asks, “What could you do?” Just past his shoulder a hand-painted sign on the pavement reads “Better View This Way!” with an arrow.
3. The boy has climbed onto a low stone wall, hands on the edge, and now he can see the stage over everyone’s heads. He is beaming. Halo, above him, says, “Someone in the Bible wanted to see Jesus like this too.”
4. A narration box reads, “Zacchaeus lived in Jericho. He was a chief tax collector and a wealthy man. One day, he heard that Jesus was passing through the city.” Zacchaeus sits at a tax table in a dark red robe and embroidered cap, a pen in his ringed hand, stacks of coins, scales and money chests in front of him. A cloth sign above reads “TAX COLLECTION” over a pair of scales. In the street beyond, a man throws up an arm and calls, “Jesus is passing through Jericho!” Halo hovers at the stall.
5. “But there was a big crowd, and Zacchaeus was too short to see over the people.” We are behind Zacchaeus in the crowd: shoulders and turbans tower over him on every side, his hands pressed against two backs, his face tilted up. Far down the street, small between the heads, Jesus walks towards the city gate.
6. “Zacchaeus was determined to see Jesus. He ran ahead of the crowd and climbed up a sycamore-fig tree.” Zacchaeus is halfway up a broad, rough-barked trunk, one sandal braced on a low wall, robe swinging, both hands gripping the bark. Below and behind him the crowd streams on towards the gate without him.
7. “He sat on a branch, looking out for Jesus who would pass below.” Seen from up in the leaves: Zacchaeus sits astride a thick branch, looking down into the street. Jesus is walking through the middle of the crowd below, clearly visible now, people on every side of him.
8. “Suddenly, Jesus looked up at Zacchaeus.” Jesus stands in the street with one hand raised towards the tree, face turned up and smiling. Zacchaeus leans out along the branch above, eyes wide, caught. Jesus says, “Zacchaeus, come down immediately. I must stay at your house today.”
9. “Zacchaeus came down at once and gladly welcomed Jesus to his home.” The two walk side by side along a sunlit street, Zacchaeus half a step ahead with one arm flung out towards his doorway, looking up at Jesus and talking. The crowd follows behind them, smiling.
10. “Zacchaeus was overjoyed. After his encounter with Jesus, he was transformed.” Inside the house: Jesus sits on a cushioned bench, Zacchaeus stands before him with both hands clasped at his chest. Shelves of chests, coins and brass vessels line the wall behind, and a low table is set with fruit.
11. “Zacchaeus stood up and said to the Lord, ‘Look, Lord! Here and now, I give half of my possessions to the poor.’” Zacchaeus stands with one hand on his chest and the other open in front of him, speaking. Jesus sits listening, and the backs of several seated guests fill the foreground.
12. “‘And if I have cheated anybody out of anything, I will pay back four times the amount.’” Zacchaeus holds out a cloth money pouch with both hands to a seated man, who reaches up to take it. Zacchaeus says, “I will pay back four times the amount.” Jesus stands smiling behind him.
13. Back in the market. The boy sits on a stone block, chin on his fist, looking up. In a thought cloud above him, Zacchaeus stands with his hands clasped, facing Jesus. The narration reads, “Meeting Jesus changed Zacchaeus. He wanted to make things right.” Halo asks, “What do you think changed Zacchaeus?” A chalkboard beside him reads “Kind People Brighter Places”.
14. “Zacchaeus showed that he was sorry by choosing to make things right.” The boy is still thinking. Three thought clouds float above him, each with a small scene and a label: “Take something that isn’t mine”, “Hurt a friend’s feelings”, and “Tell a lie”. Halo asks, “What can you do when you make a wrong choice?” A sign nearby reads “Be Kind Be Honest Be Brave”.
15. “When we make a wrong choice, we can say sorry and try to make things right.” The boy stands with his palms pressed together in apology in front of another boy who sits on a step with his arms round his knees, looking up at him. A red toy car lies on the cobbles between them. Halo says, “You can make things right, too.” A banner behind reads “A Better You A Brighter Tomorrow”.

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Primary Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 03 “ZACCHAEUS”, p.19 — “ANSWER THE QUESTIONS”*

**1. What were the shortcomings of Zacchaeus?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the two things the lesson holds against him: he was considered a sinner, and he was believed to have grown rich by taxing people as he wanted.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “He was considered a sinner by the people, as it was believed that Zacchaeus had become rich by using his position to tax people as he wanted. This made the people resentful of this man who was cheating them.” (p.18)

**2. Why was it difficult for Zacchaeus to mee Jesus?**  
*What the child must take away:* Hold both causes at once — a big crowd, and his own short stature. Neither one alone is the answer the lesson gives.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “However, there was a big crowd and Zacchaeus being of short stature could not get a glimpse of Him.” (p.18)

**3. Which tree did Zacchaeus climb to see Jesus?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the sycamore-fig tree, and know why he climbed it — he ran ahead of the crowd to see Jesus pass below.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “He ran ahead of the crowd and climbed up a sycamore-fig tree. He sat on a branch, looking out for Jesus who would pass below.” (p.18)

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Primary, Chapter 3, from the Mar Thoma Sunday School Samajam Primary Vol-1, pages 17–20. The first chapter in this library that is not a Beginner’s, and the first written to a band above the youngest — so the whole of it is calibrated one rung up: four options where Beginner gets three, a six-step sequence where Beginner gets four, wrong answers that are true sentences about the same story rather than obviously wrong ones, and a memory verse practice that begins where Beginner’s ends.

That last point is not a figure of speech. St. Luke 19: 10 is also the memory verse of Beginner Chapter 3, The Lost Coin, so the two classes can be laid side by side on the same words — which is exactly what was done, and is why the agent’s own Primary rung was set aside here. See the verse note.

Games from the Game Builder agent (`npm run agent:games -- --class primary --chapter 03`): the planner read the three curriculum questions, classified them attributes / cause / identity, and assigned reveal, multiple-choice and match. Those three are its work. The fourth and fifth are added by hand because three questions is below the Primary band’s four-to-five, and padding the three into five would have been the worse answer; what they cover is written in their notes.

Artwork downloaded from Drive (Primary / Chapter 03 / 04 - Story Artwork) and read panel by panel before a word of alt text was written; PNG to WebP at native size, no resize and no crop. Seventeen files: a portrait cover, a landscape cover and fifteen panels. Panels 6, 7 and 8 are 941×1671, 941×1671 and 942×1670 rather than the canonical 941×1672 — one or two pixels, converted without distortion and recorded rather than silently fixed.

The artwork brought a frame the draft did not have. It opens on a boy in a present-day market who cannot see over the crowd (panels 1–3), tells Zacchaeus (4–12), and returns to the same boy (13–15), where Halo asks him what he can do when he makes a wrong choice and the last panel answers it. So the eighteen text cards written before the artwork arrived are gone, replaced by the fifteen panels the chapter actually has; the narration is lettered into the art, so no card carries `text` and every alt quotes the words painted on its own panel.

One copy warning is left standing on purpose: the verse practice’s gap-fill prompt is the verse itself with a hole in it, and shortening it would mean altering the verse. Where a budget and a quotation disagree the quotation wins, and the budget reports it rather than being widened to hide it.

The book spells the name “Zacchaeus” throughout the lesson and “Zaccheus” once, in the last sentence of p.18. The artwork spells it “Zacchaeus” everywhere, which is the spelling used here.
