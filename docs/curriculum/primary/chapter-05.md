## 1. The story, beat by beat

*22 beats, the cards the app shows today, in order. This is the narrative a panel sequence has to carry.*

1. There was a man who had two sons.
2. The younger one asked his father for his share.
3. His father was sad, but divided his property between them.
4. A few days later the younger son sold his share.
5. He left home with the money and went to a far country.
6. There he wasted it all with his friends in wild living.
7. Then a great famine came, and he had nothing left.
8. His friends left him.
9. He went to work for a farmer, who sent him to look after pigs.
10. He was so hungry he could have eaten the pigs’ food.
11. But no one gave him anything.
12. Then he started thinking.
13. At his father’s house even the workers had more than enough to eat.
14. He decided to go home and say he had sinned.
15. So he set out for his father.
16. While he was still far from home, his father saw him.
17. He felt sorry for him. He ran, put his arms round him, and kissed him.
18. The son said he was no longer worthy to be called his son.
19. But his father called for the best robe, a ring and shoes.
20. He arranged a grand feast.
21. He said, “This son of mine was dead and is alive again.”
22. “He was lost and is found.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Primary Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 05 “THE HOUSE OF GOD”, p.27 — “ANSWER THE QUESTIONS”*

**1. What did the younger son demand from his father?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he asked for his share of the property.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* His share. “The younger one asked his father for his share. His father was sad but divided his property between his two sons.” (p.26)

**2. How did the younger son lose everything?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know the chain: he sold up, went far away, wasted it in wild living, and then famine came.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He spent it. “the younger son sold his property and left home with the money. He went to a far off country and wasted all his money with his friends in wild living.” Then “there was a great famine in that country, and he had nothing with him.” (p.26)

**3. How did the father receive the son when he came back?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the father ran to him and held a feast rather than punishing him.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He ran to him. “his father saw him and felt sorry for him. He ran, put his arms around his son, and kissed him.” Then the best robe, a ring, shoes and a grand feast. (pp.26–27)

## 5. The games this chapter already has

Artwork is reused as game cards, so this matters while you draw:

- a card may letter a **question**, never an **answer** a child is marked on;
- the shelf crops each card to a **centre square** and prints the game's title across the bottom of it, so any lettering must survive that crop whole and sit clear of the title;
- the whole shelf is read before any game is played, so a picture that letters **any** game's answer is barred from **every** card.

### “The Asking” · `what-he-asked-for` · multiple-choice
*Objective:* Know that the younger son asked for his share of the property.

### “How It Went” · `how-he-lost-it-all` · sequence
*Objective:* Put the losing in order, so the famine arrives after the money is already gone.  
*Shelf picture:* **none yet**

  - 2. He left for a far country
  - 3. He wasted it all

### “Coming Home” · `how-his-father-met-him` · reveal
*Objective:* Follow what the father did — saw, pitied, ran, embraced, and feasted — rather than what he might have done.  
*Shelf picture:* **none yet**  
*Must not be lettered on any card:*
  - His father saw him far off
  - He felt sorry for him
  - He ran and embraced him
  - He called for the best robe
  - He arranged a grand feast

### “True or Not?” · `lost-and-found` · true-or-not
*Objective:* Hold what the son said and what the father said — the two speeches the questions do not ask about.  
*Shelf picture:* **none yet**  
*Must not be lettered on any card:*
  - Did the father send his son away again? — no
  - Did the son say he was no longer worthy? — yes
  - Was there a feast when he came home? — yes
  - Did the father call him lost and then found? — yes
