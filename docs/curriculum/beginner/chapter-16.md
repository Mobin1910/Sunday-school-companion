## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy sits at a desk by an open window with his chin on his hand and a pencil in his fingers, an exercise book open in front of him and a dusty path winding away through the fields outside. Halo floats beside him. The caption reads, “One day, the boy was thinking about an important decision. He looked out of the window at a path and wondered which way to go.” The boy asks, “Halo, how do I know which way I should go when I don’t know what lies ahead?”
2. The same desk, Halo drifted closer and brighter. The caption reads, “Halo gently floated closer, ready to help the boy understand how God guides us.” Halo says, “Sometimes we can’t see the whole journey. But we can trust God to guide us.”
3. A glowing vision opens above the desk: an old man in a striped cloak stands with a staff raised among tents, camels and sheep in a sunlit town. The boy asks, “Can you tell me a story of someone who trusted God?” and Halo answers, “Yes! Let’s look at a story of a man who trusted God!”
4. Abram sits on a rug on his rooftop at dusk, looking up into a shaft of light over the flat-roofed town. The caption reads, “One day, while Abram was in Haran, God spoke to him. God told him to leave his home, his family, and his people, and go to a new land that He would show him.” The light says, “Leave your home, your family, and your people. Go to a land that I will show you.”
5. Abram walks out through the gate of the town with his staff, his household following — a woman, a young man, a boy — and the flocks pressing round their feet. The caption reads, “Abraham obeyed God. He left his home, his family, and his people, and began his journey to a new land.”
6. The party is out on a stony hillside now, the town small behind them, a wide valley opening ahead with mountains beyond. The caption reads, “Abraham did not know exactly where he was going, but he trusted God. So he continued his journey with his family and his people.”
7. Abram stands on a ridge at sunset with one hand shading his eyes, a boy beside him looking up, and a long green river valley spread out below. The caption reads, “Finally, they arrived at the land that God had promised.”
8. Abram holds out his arm over the valley, showing it to the boy and the people behind him. The caption reads, “Abraham looked at the beautiful land. He knew it was the land God had promised. He trusted God, even though it had been a long and difficult journey.”
9. Light breaks over the valley and Abram raises both hands into it, his family around him. The caption reads, “When Abram reached Canaan, God appeared to him and promised to give this land to his offspring.” The light says, “To your offspring I will give this land.”
10. Abram kneels and sets a last stone onto a low pile of rocks on the hillside, his household watching behind him. The caption reads, “Abram built an altar there to worship the Lord who had promised to give the land to his offspring.”
11. Abram kneels at the finished altar with both hands lifted and his face turned up to the setting sun. The caption reads, “Abram worshipped the Lord and trusted the God who had led him to this land.”
12. Back in the boy’s bedroom, a globe and a wooden cross on the shelf behind him, a big illustrated Bible open on the desk. The caption reads, “Back in his room, the boy thought about Abraham’s journey and the God who guided him.” The boy asks, “Halo, what can I learn from Abraham’s story?”
13. The boy rests his chin on both hands, listening. Halo says, “Abraham trusted God and obeyed Him, even though he didn’t know where the journey would lead. He followed God’s guidance, and God was with him every step of the way.”
14. The boy sits up, pencil in hand. Halo says, “We can also learn to trust God, even when we don’t know what will happen next. He knows the way, and He is with us just like He was with Abraham.” The boy answers, “That’s amazing! So I can trust God too, even when things are uncertain?”
15. The boy opens a hand towards Halo, smiling. He asks, “Can I worship God like Abraham did?” and Halo answers, “Of course! We can worship God through prayer, praise, and gathering with others to learn His Word.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 16 “The Journey of Abraham”, p.74 — “Tell the Answers”, questions 1 to 5*

**1. “Go to the land that I will show you”, Who said to whom?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that God said it to Abram.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* God said it to Abram. “One day God called Abram and told him ‘Go to the land that I will show you.’” (p.74)

**2. Where did Abram live?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Abram lived in the land of Harran.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* In the land of Harran. “Abram was living in the land of Harran.” (p.74)

**3. Name Abram’s wife?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Abram’s wife was Sarah.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Sarah. “He took his wife Sarah, his nephew Lot and all the possessions they had accumulated in the land of Harran.” (p.74)

**4. Which land did God promise to give to the Offspring of Abram?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that God promised the land of Canaan.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Canaan. “When they reached the land of Canaan, God appeared to Abram and said, ‘To your offspring I will give this land.’” (p.74)

**5. Why did Abram built an Altar?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Abram built altars to worship the Lord.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* To worship the Lord. “Abram built an altar there and worshipped the Lord… wherever he used to stop and set up his tent for rest, there he ensured to build an altar to the Lord and worship him.” (p.74)

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the curriculum photographs in Drive’s Beginner → Chapter 16 → “01 - Curriculum Source”: four pages, p.73 (title, prayer, Bible portion), p.74 (the lesson and the first six of “Tell the Answers”), p.75 (questions 7 and 8, and the maze) and p.76 (My Decision, the song, the Memory Verse and the Concluding Prayer). **The chapter is complete** — the first since Chapter 13 with nothing missing from Drive.

**The maze is now a game in the app.** p.75 prints “CAN YOU SHOW THE WAY?” with “(Abraham wanted to go to Canaan)” under it. It is the chapter’s own activity and there was no interaction model that could hold it, so one was built: `show-the-way`, the twelfth model, a bespoke scene alongside `pouring`, `find-the-coin`, `journey` and `provision`. The third game is that maze: the child drags Abraham along the corridor with a finger and the trail is the pencil line. It shipped as a tap-the-next-square game first and was changed; `interactions/way/ShowTheWay.tsx` records why that first call was wrong. The maze was enlarged at the same time, from a 9×9 grid of twenty steps to a 13×13 of forty-eight.

**Questions 6, 7 and 8 are not recorded in `curriculum`, and no game is built on them.** They are “Where do we gather together for worship?”, “Do we gather regularly for worship?” and “What aspects should be kept in mind when we participate for worship?” — questions about the child’s own life and habits rather than about the lesson, and the last two have no single answer at all. They belong to the teacher in the room. Recording them here would have obliged a game to answer them, and a game that marks a six-year-old on whether their family goes to church regularly is not a game this app should contain. Questions 1 to 5 are all answerable from p.74 and all five are covered.

`prayer` carries the Concluding Prayer from p.76. The opening prayer on p.73 is printed in full too — “O Lord we thank you for giving us this day to study your Word…” — but the schema holds one prayer and the closing one is what the other chapters in this class carry.

**Artwork.** Fifteen panels and both covers came from Drive’s “04 - Story Artwork”, all seventeen distinct by SHA-256, the folder enumerated by exact filename rather than by paging. The twenty-three text cards this chapter first shipped with are gone rather than reconciled, which is what happened to Beginner Chapters 5 to 15 and to Primary 1 to 3: the panels letter their own captions and speech, so a card repeating them underneath would print the same sentence twice. Each card is now a picture with `alt` describing the scene and quoting what is lettered on it.

Panel 13 is 941×1671, one pixel short of the canonical 941×1672. Converted without distortion and nothing resized it; recorded rather than fixed.

**Two places where the artwork and the printed lesson disagree. Both are the teacher’s to settle; neither is patched here.**

1. **The spelling of the place.** Panel 4 letters “while Abram was in Haran” with one r. p.74 prints “Harran” with two, twice. The lesson is the source for this app, so the first game’s board and the story’s own reference keep “Harran”, and a child who reads the panel will see the other spelling. Nothing is wrong on either side — both are used in English Bibles — but they do not match.

2. **The name, which is the chapter’s point.** The lesson ends “Since he obeyed the Lord, later the Lord changed his name from ‘Abram’ to ‘Abraham’”, and that turn is this chapter’s `celebration`. The artwork does not have it. Panels 4, 9, 10 and 11 say Abram; panels 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14 and 15 say Abraham — so he is already called Abraham while he is still walking, four panels before God appears to him in Canaan, and the moment the name changes is never drawn. The chapter still ends on it because the lesson does, but the pictures no longer show it happening.
