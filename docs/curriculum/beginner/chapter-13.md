## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy lying on his bed with a big illustrated Bible open in front of him, looking up at Halo, who glows blue and violet beside him. The boy says, “Halo! does God listen to my prayer?” and Halo answers, “Of course he does… Why do you ask?”
2. The same boy, chin on his hands and worried. A golden vision opens above him showing an elderly man and woman kneeling in prayer by candlelight. The boy says, “But it takes so long to get answers to my prayers,” and Halo says, “I know a family in the Bible who had to wait too.. Let’s see!”
3. Zechariah and Elizabeth kneeling together in their lamplit home, hands pressed in prayer, an empty wooden cradle beside them. The caption reads: “Long ago, Zechariah was a priest in the Temple of God. His wife was Elizabeth. They were both elderly. They loved God, obeyed His commandments, and prayed every day. But they had no children.” He says, “Yes. We will continue to trust Him,” and she says, “Let us keep praying to God.”
4. Zechariah at the golden altar of incense inside the Temple, hands raised in alarm, as an angel appears in a blaze of light. The caption reads: “One day, Zechariah was serving as a priest in the Temple. While he was offering incense to God, something amazing happened!” Zechariah says, “What is happening?” and the angel says, “Do not be afraid, Zechariah!”
5. The angel speaking with one hand open towards Zechariah, who clutches his chest. The caption reads: “The angel Gabriel brought Zechariah wonderful news. God had heard his prayer!” Zechariah says, “A son? But how can this happen? We are both old!” and the angel says, “Your wife Elizabeth will have a son. You must name him John!”
6. The angel raising one finger, Zechariah’s hand going to his throat. The caption reads: “Zechariah found it hard to believe the angel’s words. Gabriel told him that he would not be able to speak until the promise came true.” Zechariah begins, “But…” and the angel says, “Because you did not believe my words, you will not be able to speak until this happens.”
7. Zechariah on the Temple steps with a crowd pressing round him, one hand at his throat and the other raised, unable to answer. The caption reads: “When Zechariah came out of the Temple, he could not speak. The people were surprised, so he used signs to tell them what had happened.” Someone asks, “Zechariah, what happened inside?”
8. Elizabeth sitting up in bed holding a newborn wrapped in cream cloth, Zechariah leaning over them and neighbours smiling behind. The caption reads: “After some time, Elizabeth gave birth to a baby boy, just as the angel had promised!”
9. The room full of relatives, Elizabeth holding the baby and looking up. The caption reads: “When it was time to name the baby, everyone expected him to be named after his father.” A neighbour says, “Let us name him Zechariah, after his father!” and Elizabeth answers, “No! He shall be called John.”
10. Zechariah seated with a writing tablet on his knee and a stylus in his hand, the baby in Elizabeth’s arms beside him and the neighbours leaning in. The caption reads: “The people asked Zechariah what the baby should be called. Since he could not speak, he asked for a writing tablet.” Someone asks, “Zechariah, what name should we give the baby?”
11. Zechariah standing with both arms raised and his mouth open, the tablet set down, the crowd around him amazed. The caption reads: “As soon as Zechariah wrote, ‘His name is John,’ he could speak again! He began praising God, and everyone was amazed.” He says, “Praise be to God!” and a neighbour says, “What wonderful things God has done!”
12. Back in the boy’s bedroom at night with the Bible open in front of him, the rejoicing family still glowing above. The caption reads: “Zechariah and Elizabeth waited a long time, but God heard their prayers. Their story reminds us to keep trusting God.” The boy says, “Now I understand! I can keep praying even when I have to wait,” and Halo answers, “That’s right! God hears your prayers. Keep trusting Him.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 13 “ZECHARIAH AND ELIZABETH”, pp.60–61 — “TELL THE ANSWERS” and “CAN YOU SAY?”*

**1. What are the features of Zechariah’s family?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that theirs was a family that lived with God.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* They lived with God. “Theirs was a family that lived with God. They used to pray to God every day. They only did good to others. They did not fail to obey God’s commandments. No one had anything bad to say about them.” (p.60)

**2. What was Zechariah and Elizabeth’s problem?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that they had no children.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* They had no children. “But they had a problem. They had no children.” (p.60)

**3. What did they do about their problem?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that they cried and prayed to God regularly for a baby.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* They prayed. “They cried and prayed to God regularly for a baby.” (p.60)

**4. What was the name of the angel who appeared to Zechariah?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the angel was Gabriel.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Gabriel. “Suddenly the God’s angel Gabriel appeared before Zechariah.” (p.60)

**5. What did the angel of God say to Zechariah?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know the angel’s words — do not be afraid, God has heard, Elizabeth will have a son, name him John.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “Don’t be afraid, Zechariah! God has heard your prayer. Your wife Elizabeth will give birth to a son. Name him John.” (p.60)

**6. What are the names of John the Baptist’s parents?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that they were Zechariah and Elizabeth.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Zechariah and Elizabeth. “Zechariah was a priest in the Jerusalem temple. His wife’s name was Elizabeth.” … “That baby grew up to be John the Baptist.” (p.60)

**7. Who baptized Jesus?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that John the Baptist baptized Jesus.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* John the Baptist. “That baby grew up to be John the Baptist, who baptized Jesus.” (p.60)

**8. What did the angel say regarding naming the baby?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the angel said to name him John.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “Name him John.” (p.60) And it happened: “As the angel had said, they named the child John.”

**9. What happened to Zechariah when he doubted angel’s words?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he was unable to speak until it happened.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He lost his speech. “Because you do not believe this word of mine, you will not be able to speak until it happens.” … “when Zechariah came out, he was unable to speak.” (p.60)

**10. When did Zechariah get back his ability to speak?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he spoke again at the moment they named the child John.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* When the baby was named. “As the angel had said, they named the child John. At that moment, Zechariah opened his mouth and spoke again.” (p.60)

**11. What are the features that our family should have?**  
*What the child must take away:* Meet what the lesson says a family that lives with God looks like — the same features as Zechariah’s.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* **This question is put to the class and has no printed answer.** What the chapter offers is the list it gives for Zechariah’s family on p.60, which is why the game shows that list rather than marking anything.

**12. Who gives children to the parents?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that children come from God.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* God. The lesson has God hearing their prayer and giving them a son, and the chapter’s own memory verse on p.62 says it outright: “Sons are a heritage from the Lord, children a reward from him.” (Psalm 127:3)

The chapter’s “06 - Memory Verse” folder is empty, which is right for this class: Beginner prints its memory verse on the chapter’s own closing page, and Psalm 127:3 was read from p.62.

**The artwork is in.** Fourteen files from “04 - Story Artwork” — a cover, a landscape cover and panels 1 to 12 — PNG to WebP at native size, no resize and no crop, and all fourteen distinct. Panel 11 is uploaded as “Panel 11.png” with a capital P; the importer reads the number rather than the case, so it landed correctly.

**The twenty-one text cards are gone.** Every panel is lettered, captions and speech bubbles both, so the app writing the same sentence underneath would say it twice and the caption layout would crop the art to make room. The story is twelve picture-only cards, one per panel, each with alt text that describes the scene and quotes what is written in it — the same thing Chapters 5 to 12 did when their artwork arrived.

**The artwork brought a present-day frame with it.** Panels 1, 2 and 12 are a boy, his Bible and Halo in a bedroom; Zechariah’s story runs from panel 3 to panel 11 between them. The frame is this chapter’s own question — why answers take so long — and panel 12 answers it.

**One thing for the artist:** the portrait cover is 982×1602 where every panel is 941×1672. Recorded on the cover block.

**All four games carry a shelf picture.** A shelf card is read before the game is played, so it may letter a question or a trouble but never an answer a child will be marked on. That rules out most of this chapter: panel 3 letters both of game 1’s choosing answers, panels 5 and 6 letter “Gabriel”, and panels 6, 7, 9, 10 and 11 each letter a step of game 3’s sequence. What is left — panels 1, 2, 4, 8 and 12 — is what the games carry, and panel 1 was dropped because the shelf’s square crop cuts both its bubbles mid-sentence.

**Twelve questions and four games.** Four is this band’s ceiling and twelve is the longest list this class has had, so the games carry two or three interactions each and name every question they answer. The grouping follows the lesson’s own joints: the family and its one sorrow, the angel and everything he said, the months Zechariah could not speak, and who was who.

Questions 11 and 12 come from “CAN YOU SAY?” rather than “TELL THE ANSWERS”. Question 11 asks a child what their own family should be like and has no printed answer, so its game shows the lesson’s list and marks nothing.

The chapter has two prayers and both are printed in full, unlike Chapter 12 where both were stage directions. The opener on p.59 is “Dear God who hears our prayers, teach us to pray properly. Amen” — the book prints no full stop after that Amen — and the close on p.62 is the one carried in `prayer` below.
