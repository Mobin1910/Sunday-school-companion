## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy walking a sunlit street with his mother, a backpack on his shoulders and one hand up to shade his eyes; bougainvillea over a wall, palms beyond, and the sun blazing in the corner. The boy says, “It’s so hot!” and Halo, floating beside him, asks, “What helps you when the sun is hot?”
2. The two of them sitting on a low wall in the shade of a big tree, the mother holding a water bottle. The boy grins up and says, “Ahh! The shade feels nice!” and Halo answers, “Yes! Shade helps protect us from the hot sun.”
3. Dusk, the sky orange over the rooftops. The mother crouches with a lit flashlight and the boy crouches beside her in its beam. He says, “A flashlight!” and Halo says, “Yes! A light helps us see in the dark.”
4. The same street at nightfall, and a vision opens across the top of the panel: a long line of robed travellers winding through red desert mountains. The mother, chin on her hand, says, “I wonder what people did long ago…”, Halo asks, “Would you like to find out?” and the boy answers, “Yes!”
5. The desert itself. A great column of people winds away over the hills — a bearded man leading with a staff, a child holding his hand, women carrying bundles, a donkey loaded with wood — and the line runs on to the horizon. The caption reads, “Long ago, God’s people, the Israelites, were travelling through a great desert.”
6. The same march under a white sun, and everybody is shielding their eyes. The man at the front has a hand to his brow, a child squints, a woman covers a smaller child’s head. The caption reads, “The desert was very hot. There were no trees to give them shade.”
7. An enormous white cloud rolls across the sky above the column, and the people look up at it — one child pointing, the faces no longer screwed up against the light. The caption reads, “So, God sent them a cloud. The cloud reduced the heat of the sun.”
8. Night over the mountains, a full moon, and the column walking on in the dark. The caption reads, “But at night, they needed light.”
9. A pillar of fire stands in the night sky, lighting the whole valley orange, and the people walk on looking up at it with their faces lit. The caption reads, “Then God prepared a pillar of fire to give them light.”
10. Both at once, and the panel is split between them: the white pillar of cloud standing in a bright sky on the left, the pillar of fire burning against the night on the right, and the column walking between. Two captions: “The pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire continued to lead them,” and “God was with them.”
11. Back on the boy’s own front steps at night, the porch lamp on behind him. He sits with his chin on his hand and says, “God took care of the Israelites…”, and Halo asks, “Do you think God cares for you too?”
12. The same steps a moment later. The boy has both fists up and is beaming. He says, “Yes! God is with me too!” and Halo answers, “Just like He cared for the Israelites.”
13. The boy walking home along a lamplit pavement with his backpack on, Halo bright beside him. He says, “I will not forget God who is with me as support and shade,” and Halo says, “And He guides you every day.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 10 “PILLAR OF CLOUD AND FIRE”, p.49 — “TELL THE ANSWERS” and “MARK IT AS RIGHT OR WRONG”*

**1. Who were the people that traveled through the desert for a long time?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the Israelites as the people on the journey.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The Israelites. “…the Israelites were left with no food in the desert… These people continued this journey for many years.” (p.48)

**2. How they were protected from the heat/sun, during the day?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that God sent the cloud, and that it shaded them by day.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* God sent them the clouds. “So, God sent them the clouds. The clouds reduced the heat of the day.” (p.48)

**3. How was the ‘pillar of fire’ helpful to them?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the pillar of fire gave them light at night.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* It gave them light at night. “God prepared blazes in the sky, pillar of fire to give them light.” (p.48)

**4. How did the Israelites recognize God’s presence?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire were the signs of God being with them.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* By the pillar of cloud and the pillar of fire. “They understood that God would love, care for and protect His people.” (p.48), and p.49 prints it outright: “The Pillar of Cloud and Pillar of Fire were seen as the signs of God’s presence, by the Israelites.”

**5. The Israelites traveled in the desert for only a few days.**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that this is Wrong — the journey went on for many years.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Wrong. “These people continued this journey for many years.” (p.48)

**6. A Pillar of Cloud protected them during the day in the desert.**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that this is Right — the cloud is the daytime half of the pair.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Right. “So, God sent them the clouds. The clouds reduced the heat of the day.” (p.48)

**7. A Pillar of Fire helped them at night.**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that this is Right — the fire is the night-time half of the pair.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Right. “…pillar of fire to give them light.” (p.48)

**8. The Pillar of Cloud and Pillar of Fire were seen as the signs of God’s presence, by the Israelites.**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that this is Right — the two pillars are how they knew God was with them.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Right. The statement is the book’s own, printed on p.49.

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the four curriculum photographs in Drive’s “01 - Curriculum Source” for Beginner / Chapter 10, read at full size after rotating them upright: the opener (p.47), the lesson (p.48), the exercises (p.49) and the close (p.50). All four pages are complete and nothing runs off an edge.

Artwork downloaded from “04 - Story Artwork” and read panel by panel before a word of alt text was written; PNG to WebP at native size, no resize and no crop. Every file is exactly 941×1672 or its transpose.

**One panel is missing and it is not a mistake in this file.** Drive holds sixteen files, but “panel 3.png” is byte for byte identical to “panel 2.png”, so one of the two never uploaded and fifteen were imported with `--partial`. The story runs 1, 2, 4 … 14. Which of the pair is the real one is settled by the panels either side; see the note on panel 2. Chapter 6 lost a panel the same way.

The artwork brought the book’s present-day frame with it — a walk in the sun, a flashlight at dusk, and the boy’s own street at the end — where the text draft had only gestured at it. The seventeen text cards are gone rather than reconciled, which is what happened to Beginner Chapters 5 to 9 and to Primary 1 to 3.

Every game names a shelf picture and none carries artwork inside it. That is a fact about this chapter rather than a shortfall: panels 6, 8, 10 and 11 each letter an answer to one of the four games, and the match’s third pair — manna and quail — is a recall of Chapter 4 that this chapter never draws, so a half-illustrated match would have been worse than a plain one.

The book’s “LET’S WORK: Fill the missing Letters/Words” has no game here. It is a pencil-and-paper task — writing D into ⬚AY and G into NI⬚HT — and Chapter 8 set the precedent that those stay on the page they are printed on.
