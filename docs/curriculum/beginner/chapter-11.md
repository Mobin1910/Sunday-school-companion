1. A boy sitting cross-legged on a rug in a warm lamplit bedroom, chin on his hand, looking up at Halo glowing blue and violet beside him. A football and picture books on the floor. A sign above reads “God hears our prayers!”, and Halo asks, “Have you ever prayed for something you really wanted?”
2. The same room. Halo asks, “What do you do while you wait?” and the boy, hands clasped together and smiling up, answers, “I keep praying and wait for God.”

4. Manoah and his wife kneeling side by side on a patterned rug, heads bowed and hands pressed together, water jars around them and an archway behind opening onto hills and a village. 5. Manoah’s wife kneeling in a sunlit courtyard, looking up at an angel in white robes standing in the light with one hand open towards her. The caption reads: “One day, an angel of the Lord appeared to Manoah’s wife.” The angel says, “You will have a son.”

7. Manoah and his wife kneeling in prayer again, seen wider, a lamp lit behind them and the hills going gold at evening. The caption above repeats how they cried and prayed, and a second box at the foot reads: “God heard their prayers.”

9. Samson grown up, tall and broad in a sleeveless tunic with a wide belt, his mother and father standing either side looking up at him proudly. The caption reads: “They raised Samson just as the angel had told them. God blessed Samson. He grew up to be a handsome and strong young man. There were no strong people like him anywhere.”
10. Samson standing alone on a hillside above a river valley, light falling across him. The caption reads: “The Spirit of God was with him. He was so strong that he could tear apart a lion that came roaring at him.”
11. A lion leaping down over the rocks with its mouth wide open and claws out; Samson turning to meet it, braced on the stones. The caption reads: “One day, a fierce lion came roaring at Samson.”
12. Samson gripping the lion’s jaws with both hands and forcing them apart, teeth bared with the effort, dust kicking up around them. The caption reads: “The Spirit of God gave Samson great strength. Samson tore the lion apart with his bare hands.”
13. Samson lifting an armoured Philistine giant clean over his head while other soldiers fall back below a hilltop fort, shields and helmets scattered on the ground. The caption reads: “Samson defeated the Philistine giants. What a strength!”
14. Samson reclining at a torchlit feast with a cup in his hand, laughing men leaning in around a table of food and jugs. The caption reads: “But when Samson grew up, he disobeyed his parents and God. He fell into bad company.”
15. Samson on his knees in a courtyard, heavy chains on both wrists, armed guards standing over him with spears, his head bowed and his hair cut short. The caption reads: “The Spirit of God left him. His strength was then lost. So later Samson was weak enough to be defeated.”
16. Back in the bedroom at night. The boy sits with an open picture book, hands turned up, asking “What gifts has God given me?” Halo answers, “God has given you many talents too!”
17. The boy holding up a crayon drawing of a church on a green hill under a yellow sun, crayons and bricks around him. Halo asks, “How can you use your talents?” and the boy answers, “I can use my gifts to help others and obey God!”
18. The boy drying a plate at the kitchen table beside his mother, his father leaning in behind them and Halo glowing at the end of the table. The caption reads: “God gives us gifts and blessings. We can use them to love, help, and obey Him.” His father says, “God has given you gifts. You can use them to love and help your family,” and the boy says, “I want to use my gifts for good!”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 11 “SAMSON”, p.53 — “ANSWER THE QUESTIONS”*

**1. What was the name of Manoah’s son?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that Manoah’s son was named Samson.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Samson. “After some time, Manoah’s wife gave birth to a son. He was a lovely baby. They named him Samson.” (p.52)

**2. To whom did the angel appear?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the angel appeared to Manoah’s wife.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* To Manoah’s wife. “One day, an angel appeared to Manoah’s wife.” (p.52) She then “told Manoah what the angel had said”.

**3. What did the angel say to Manoah’s wife?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know the angel’s words — a son, dedicated to God from birth, no wine, never cut his hair, and he would rescue Israel from the Philistines.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “You will give birth to a son. From the day of his birth, he will be dedicated to God. Be sure not to drink any wine. You must never cut his hair. He will rescue Israel from the Philistines.” (p.52)

**4. Who came to fight against the children of Israel?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the Philistines came for war against Israel and caused great damage.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The Philistines. “In those days, it was common for the Philistines to come for war against Israel and cause great damage on them.” (p.52)

**5. What was special about Samson?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he was dedicated to God from birth, drank no wine, never cut his hair, and would rescue Israel.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He was dedicated to God. The angel’s terms on p.52 are the whole of it — given to God from the day of his birth, no wine, hair never cut — and “they raised Samson just as angel of God had told them”.

**6. Can you give examples that demonstrate the strength of Samson?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he tore apart the lion that came roaring at him and defeated the Philistine giants.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Two are printed. “He was so strong that, he tore apart the lion that came roaring at him. He defeated the Philistine giants.” (p.52) The page adds “There were no strong people like him anywhere.”

**7. What made Samson strong?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the Spirit of God was with him.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The Spirit of God. “The Spirit of God was with him.” (p.52) The same sentence read backwards is the end of the chapter: “The Spirit of God left him. His strength was then lost.”

**8. Why did Samson have to mourn later?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that he disobeyed his parents and God, fell into bad company, the Spirit of God left him, and his strength was lost.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He disobeyed. “But when he grew up, he disobeyed his parents and God. He fell into bad company. The Spirit of God left him. His strength was then lost. So later Samson was weak enough to be defeated.” (p.52)

**9. What are the things that should be followed by those who are set apart for God?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know what the angel asked of Samson, and what Samson later let go of.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* **This question is put to the class and has no printed answer.** What the chapter gives is the angel’s asks on p.52 — belong to God, drink no wine, never cut his hair — and, read against the ending, the two Samson let go of: obedience to his parents and to God, and keeping clear of bad company.

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the four curriculum photographs in Drive’s “01 - Curriculum Source” for Beginner / Chapter 11 — the opener (p.51), the lesson (p.52), the nine questions with the colouring page (p.53) and the close (p.54). All four are around 2 MB and were read by eye at full size.

The chapter’s “06 - Memory Verse” folder is empty, which is right for this class: Beginner prints its memory verse on the chapter’s own closing page, and Psalm 6:9 was read from p.54.

**The artwork is in.** Twenty files from “04 - Story Artwork” — a cover, a landscape cover and panels 1 to 18 — imported at native size, all twenty distinct (no duplicate upload of the kind Chapters 6 and 10 had).

**The twenty-two text cards are gone.** Every panel is lettered — captions and speech bubbles both — so the app writing the same sentence underneath would say it twice, and the reader’s caption layout crops the art to make room for the repeat. The story is now eighteen picture-only cards, one per panel, each with alt text that describes the scene and quotes what is written in it. That is what Beginner Chapters 5 to 10 and Primary 1 to 3 all did when their artwork arrived.

**The artwork has its own framing.** Panels 1–3 and 16–18 are a boy and a glowing companion in a bedroom, asking about prayer at the front and about gifts at the back, with the Samson story held between them in panels 4–15. The story cards follow that structure rather than starting cold at Manoah.

**Three things for the artist.**

1. *Panels 16, 17 and 18 are 1024×1536, where every other panel is 941×1672.* They were converted without distortion and they are the three closing framing panels, so it looks like that set was made at a different size rather than one slipping through. They will not crop the same way as the rest.
2. *Panels 4 and 7 carry nearly the same caption.* Panel 4 puts all five sentences in one box; panel 7 splits the last one, “God heard their prayers.”, into a second box at the foot. Both are used here and the repetition is made to work — panel 4 is the praying and panel 7 is the answer, after the angel has come — but the repeated top box reads as a leftover and is worth a look.
3. *Every panel is lettered*, captions and speech bubbles both. That is lovely in the reader and it is the one real constraint on using the art inside games.

**Which panels the games may use.** A shelf card is read before the game is played, so it may letter a question or a trouble but never an answer a child will be marked on. By that rule most of this chapter is out: panels 5, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14 and 15 each spell an answer in words, and panel 10 and panel 12 both say “the Spirit of God”, which is the answer to the choosing round inside game 3. What is left is the questions in panels 1, 2, 3, 16, 17 and 18 and the troubles in panels 4, 7 and 11.

Of those, the shelf card is a square centre crop, so a panel whose bubble sits just above the crop line gets cut mid-word — panels 3, 16 and 17 all do. The four the games carry are the ones that are both safe and whole: 4 and 11 have no lettering inside the crop at all, and 1 and 18 keep their bubbles entire. Unlettered variants of panels 5, 6, 14 and 15 would let the games show the moments they are actually about.

The art’s closing panels are about gifts and talents, while the book’s My Decision on p.54 is about being a blessing to one’s parents. They sit well enough together and the decision card is unchanged, but they are not quite the same ending.

**Nine questions and four games.** This class is allowed three to four games, and nine questions will not go into four one at a time, so the games carry two or three interactions each and name every question they answer. The grouping follows the lesson’s own joints: the angel’s visit, the angel’s words, Samson’s strength, and the losing of it.

**The chapter ends badly, and it is not softened.** The book says plainly that Samson disobeyed, that the Spirit of God left him, and that he was defeated. That is on the cards and in the fourth game. What is not carried over is the book’s “one may lose one’s head” register — there is none here to carry — and the celebration line turns to the part a six-year-old is being asked to hold: that he was a prayed-for child, given to his parents by God, which is this chapter’s own My Decision.

The chapter has two prayers. The opener on p.51 is “Dear God, we praise you for the grace that has brought us together today to experience your word. Amen.”; the Concluding Prayer on p.54 is the one carried in `prayer` below.
