## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy in a red shirt sits on a low wall at the edge of a school playground with his leg bandaged, another boy crouching beside him and Halo glowing behind them, children playing football in the background. The hurt boy says, “My ankle hurts. I can’t play today.” and his friend asks, “Are you okay? Why aren’t you playing?”
2. The friend sits alone on the wall with his chin on his hand, thinking, Halo floating beside him. He says, “I wish I could do something to help him!” and Halo answers, “Let’s discover a story about two friends who helped someone in need.”
3. A man in a torn brown cloak sits on the ground against the temple wall with his hand held out, his crutch beside him, pilgrims passing by. The caption reads, “Every day, a man who could not walk was carried to the Beautiful Gate of the temple to beg.” He says, “Please give me some money.”
4. Two men stop in front of him — one in a blue cloak reaching out a hand, the other behind in green. The caption reads, “As Peter and John were going to the temple to pray, the man looked at them, expecting to receive something.” Peter says, “Look at us!” and the beggar says, “Please give me some money.”
5. Peter leans down with an open hand while the man reaches up from the ground. The caption reads, “But Peter and John did not give him money. Instead, Peter said something unexpected.” Peter says, “I don’t have any silver or gold for you.” and “But I have something much better. In the name of Jesus Christ, stand up and walk!”
6. Peter has taken the man by the hand and is pulling him up off the ground. The caption reads, “Peter reached out, took the man by the hand, and helped him to stand up.” Peter says, “In the name of Jesus Christ, stand up!” and the man asks, “What really?”
7. The man is on his feet with both arms thrown up, mid-leap, Peter watching beside him. The caption reads, “Right away, the man’s feet and ankles became strong. He stood up, walked and even jumped!” The man shouts, “I can walk! I can walk!” and Peter answers, “All glory to Jesus Christ!”
8. The man walks forward with both hands lifted, Peter beside him and a crowd gathering behind. The caption reads, “The man who had never walked began to walk! He jumped, praised God and went into the temple with Peter and John.” He says, “Thank you, God! I can walk!”
9. The man is jumping with one fist in the air, Peter and John each side of him and the crowd pressing in. The caption reads, “The man who had been lame his whole life was now walking, jumping and praising God!” He says, “Thank you, God! Thank you!”
10. Back at the playground, the friend turns to the hurt boy on the bench with an open hand, Halo between them. He says, “I can’t heal your ankle, but I can pray for you!” and Halo answers, “That’s kind! We can pray for people and care for them, too.”
11. Both boys sit on the bench with their eyes closed and their hands folded, Halo glowing above them. The caption reads, “So, the boy prayed for his friend, asking Jesus to help him heal. He also decided to be a good friend and care for him.” Halo says, “Jesus hears our prayers! Let’s also keep showing love and care for our friends.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 19 “Healing - In the Name of Jesus”, p.89 — “Tell the Answers”*

**1. At which temple gate was the crippled man begging?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that the man begged at the temple gate called Beautiful.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The gate called Beautiful. “…a man who was lame from birth was being carried to the temple gate called Beautiful, where he was put every day to beg…” (p.88)

**2. Which of Jesus’ disciples came to the temple?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Peter and John came to the temple.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Peter and John. “One day Peter and John were going up to the temple at the time of prayer—at three in the afternoon.” (p.88)

**3. What did Peter say to the lame man.?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Peter had no silver or gold and told him to walk in the name of Jesus.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “Silver or gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk.” (p.88)

**4. In whose name do we pray.?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that we pray in the name of Jesus.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* In the name of Jesus. “In the name of Jesus they healed him.” (p.88)

## 5. The games this chapter already has

Artwork is reused as game cards, so this matters while you draw:

- a card may letter a **question**, never an **answer** a child is marked on;
- the shelf crops each card to a **centre square** and prints the game's title across the bottom of it, so any lettering must survive that crop whole and sit clear of the title;
- the whole shelf is read before any game is played, so a picture that letters **any** game's answer is barred from **every** card.

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the curriculum photographs the teacher added for Chapter 19: four pages, p.87 (title, prayer, Bible portion), p.88 (the lesson), p.89 (“Tell the Answers” and a “Colour the picture” activity) and p.90 (My Decision, the song, the Memory Verse and the Concluding Prayer). **The chapter is complete** — nothing missing, as in Chapter 16.

**The four photographs are sitting directly in the Chapter 19 folder rather than inside its “01 - Curriculum Source” subfolder, which is empty.** They were read where they are and nothing in Drive was moved or renamed. Worth knowing only because the folder that usually holds them looks empty from the outside.

Four games, four of four questions covered, two of four choose-one-of-N. The third answers no printed question and the gates note it; its own note says why it earns its place.

`prayer` carries the Concluding Prayer from p.90. The opening prayer on p.87 is printed in full as well, but the schema holds one and the closing one is what most of this class carries.

**The Memory Verse is printed twice in this chapter and the two copies differ by one word** — see the verse note. It is an editorial question for the teacher and nothing here guesses at it.

The stray full stops before the question marks — “there.?” in the lesson, “man.?” and “pray.?” in the questions — are the book’s and are carried verbatim.

**Artwork.** Eleven panels and both covers came from Drive’s “04 - Story Artwork”, all thirteen distinct by SHA-256, the folder enumerated by exact filename rather than by paging. The twenty-eight text cards this chapter first shipped with are gone rather than reconciled, which is what happened to Beginner Chapters 5 to 18 and to Primary 1 to 3. Each card is now a picture with `alt` describing the scene and quoting what is lettered on it.

The frame story is a new one for this class and worth noting: it is set in a school playground, with a boy whose ankle is hurt and a friend who wants to help. The chapter ends not with a healing but with the friend praying for him — “I can’t heal your ankle, but I can pray for you!” — which is a more careful landing than this story could have had.

**The artwork gives Peter a different line from the one the book prints, and it is the memory verse.** p.88 and p.90 both have “In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk.” Panel 5 letters “But I have something much better. In the name of Jesus Christ, stand up and walk!” and panel 6 letters “In the name of Jesus Christ, stand up!” — both drop “of Nazareth” and both add “stand up”. The verse and the story cards follow the book; the panels are carried as drawn. A child who learns the verse and then reads the panel will see two wordings, so this is worth the teacher’s decision rather than a silent patch. It is the third chapter running where a panel and the lesson differ — Chapter 16 on Abraham’s name, Chapter 18 on the last line — and the first where the difference touches the Memory Verse itself.

**Shelf cards were tight here.** Of the eleven panels only 3, 4, 7, 8 and 9 survive the square centre crop with their lettering whole; the other six have a bubble cut mid-sentence. Panel 7 then had to be barred on content — it letters “All glory to Jesus Christ!”, and naming Jesus is the fourth game’s answer — which left exactly four cards for four games. Every game has one.
