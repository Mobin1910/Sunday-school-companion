## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy in a red T-shirt sits on a grassy hillside under a leafy tree, arms round his knees, looking up at a bright blue sky full of clouds. A lake and a village lie below him, mountains beyond, a butterfly and daisies at his feet. Halo floats beside him and says, “Look up!” The boy answers, “Wow!” The caption reads, “What do you see in the sky?”
2. The boy is on his feet on the same hillside, pointing up at the sun blazing above the clouds, birds crossing the sky and a squirrel on the fence behind him. He says, “Look! The Sun!” and Halo answers, “Yes! And the clouds!” The caption reads, “The Sun gives us light all around us.”
3. The same hillside at dusk. The sun has dropped behind the mountains, the sky is orange fading to deep blue, a thin crescent moon and the first stars are out, and the lights of the town are coming on along the lake. The boy says, “The Sun is gone!” and Halo says, “Now it is night.” The caption reads, “When the Sun sets, darkness comes. We call this time night.”
4. Full night. A big round moon and a sky thick with stars over the dark mountains, the town glittering by the water. The boy points up and says, “The Moon and stars!” after Halo asks, “What do we see at night?” The caption reads, “At night, we see the Moon and many twinkling stars.”
5. Daylight again, and the boy has turned away from the sky. He stands with both arms open beside a stream, a rainbow arching over the mountains behind him, a deer drinking, a rabbit in the grass, a butterfly and flowers around his feet. Halo says, “The sky is beautiful! But look around you…” and the boy answers, “Wow! So many things!” The caption reads, “Look around! We can see many kinds of plants, animals, and water.”
6. The boy stands by the waterfall with a hand on his chin, thinking. A squirrel watches from the branch above, a rabbit sits in the flowers, a deer drinks downstream. He asks, “Who made all these?” and Halo says, “Hmm… who do you think?” The caption reads, “Who created all these?”
7. The boy throws both arms wide, beaming, in front of the waterfall and the mountains. Halo says, “God!” and the boy says, “Yes! God created all these!” The caption reads, “God created everything.”
8. The boy kneels and drops a plastic bottle into a green recycling bin beside the stream; more litter lies on the ground and a rabbit watches. A small wooden sign reads “A clean earth is a happy home!” Halo asks, “God made this beautiful world. How can we take care of it?” and the boy answers, “We can keep it clean!” The caption reads, “We should take care of God’s beautiful creation.”
9. The boy stands with both arms flung wide beside the waterfall, a deer and a rabbit nearby. He says, “I will love God’s creation!” and Halo adds, “And take good care of it!” The caption reads, “We can love God by caring for His creation.”
10. The boy sits on the hillside again at sunset, Halo beside him, and above them float seven little thought clouds holding everything he has seen: the sun, a night sky with the moon and stars, a rainbow, a waterfall, flowers, a bluebird and a deer. He says, “Wow! We discovered so many wonderful things!” Halo says, “God made them all!” and then, “Let’s see what you remember!” The caption reads, “God made the beautiful world around us.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 06 “GOD’S HANDICRAFT”, p.33 — “ANSWER THE QUESTIONS”*

**1. What gives us light in the day?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the sun, and know that the time we can see it is what we call day.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The sun. “The sun gives light all around us. We call the time when we can see the sun as day.” (p.32)

**2. What gives us light in the night?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the moon — what the lesson says we see once the sun has set.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The moon. “We see the moon, many small and big twinkling stars, etc. at night.” (p.32)

**3. What else can we see in the sky at night?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name the stars, small and big — and the rainbow, among the sky’s endless sights.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “Many small and big twinkling stars, etc. at night.” The lesson also asks, “Have you seen the rainbow in the sky?” (p.32)

**4. Name two flowers you like**  
*What the child must take away:* The child’s own answer, said out loud. The point is looking at creation, not recalling a list.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The lesson names no flowers. It says only “Many varieties of plants, animals, and water bodies…!” (p.32), so the answer here is the child’s and the book does not supply one.

**5. Name two animals you like**  
*What the child must take away:* The child’s own answer, said out loud.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The lesson names no animals — see question 4. The answer is the child’s.

**6. Name two birds you like**  
*What the child must take away:* The child’s own answer, said out loud.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The lesson names no birds — see question 4. The answer is the child’s.

**7. Who created everything?**  
*What the child must take away:* God. The lesson’s own one-word answer, and the point the whole chapter turns on.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* God. “Who created all these? God!” (p.32)

**8. How do we give thanks to creator God?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember Him when we see nature, be grateful, worship and glorify Him — and keep nature beautiful.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “We should remember the creator God. We should always be grateful to God, who created everything. We should worship and glorify God the creator. Along with that, it is our responsibility to keep our nature beautiful.” (p.32)

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Beginners, Chapter 6, from the Mar Thoma Sunday School Samajam Beginners Vol-1, pages 31–34. Not a story: the lesson is the sky and what is in it, and the chapter's whole argument is that looking up should make you think of who made it.

Eight questions, more than any chapter here has had, and three of them are a shape this library had never met: “Name two flowers you like” asks the child rather than the lesson. They are covered by reveals, which is the one mechanic with no wrong answer in it. See the curriculum note.

Games from the Game Builder agent (`npm run agent:games -- --class beginner --chapter 06`). Its first run sent all eight questions to `unsure` and handed back four multiple-choices — the outcome that agent exists to prevent — so the classifier gained “who…”, “what gives…”, “what else…”, “how do we…” and a new `personal` category, with the whole set in the selftest’s regression table. Its second run planned the four games below. One mechanic was overridden by hand and the reason is in that game’s note.

Artwork downloaded from Drive (Beginner / Chapter 06 / 04 - Story Artwork) and read panel by panel before a word of alt text was written; PNG to WebP at native size, no resize and no crop. The narration and the speech are lettered into the art, so no card carries `text` and every alt quotes the words painted on its own panel. The sixteen text cards written before the artwork arrived are gone.

**Two panels are missing from Drive and this chapter ships with the gap.** The artwork numbers 1 to 12; the folder holds 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, and its “Panel 3.png” is a byte-for-byte copy of panel 2 rather than a panel. What panel 3 almost certainly says is “We call this time day”, so as it stands a child is told what night is called and never what day is called — in a chapter whose first game is Day and Night. Panel 5 sits where the lesson asks “Have you looked at the sky at night?” and its absence does not show. Adding them later is additive: cards name their picture, so nothing renumbers.

The portrait cover is 981×1602 rather than the canonical 941×1672 — a squarer frame than every other cover in the library. Converted without distortion and recorded rather than silently fixed; it is cropped by both screens that show a cover anyway.

The book prints “Learn to sing (song)” followed by “(Sing a suitable song)” and names none, so this chapter has no `song` block. Choosing one would be putting words in the curriculum’s mouth, which is the same class of mistake as guessing a verse.
