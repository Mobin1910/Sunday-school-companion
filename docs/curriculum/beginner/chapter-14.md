## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy in pyjamas sits on the edge of his bed with his chin on his hand and a toy dog in his arms, Halo glowing beside him. Through the open door his mother lies ill in bed while someone sits with her and strokes her hair. The boy says, “I hope they get better soon.”
2. The boy sits with his hands clasped between his knees, looking up at Halo. He says, “Halo, I wish I could do something to help Mum.” Halo answers, “You can pray to God and ask Him to help her.”
3. A glowing vision opens above the boy’s bedroom: a bearded king lies in a carved bed with his crown set on the table beside him. The boy asks, “Will praying actually help?” Halo answers, “Yes! Let’s check out a king in the Bible who did the same.”
4. King Hezekiah stands on a palace balcony in red and purple robes and a gold crown, looking up at the sky, with a lion banner beside him and the walled city of Judah on the hills behind. The caption reads, “Long ago, in the land of Judah, there lived a king named Hezekiah. He loved God and tried to do what was right.”
5. The king lies back on his pillows under a heavy quilt, pale and looking up, his crown set aside on the table. The caption reads, “One day, King Hezekiah became very sick. He was so ill that he was near death.”
6. An old prophet in a green cloak stands at the foot of the bed with one hand raised, speaking to the king. The caption reads, “The prophet Isaiah came to visit the sick king with a message from the Lord.” Isaiah says, “This is what the Lord says: Put your house in order, because you are going to die; you will not recover.”
7. The king has rolled over to face the stone wall, hands pressed together and tears on his cheek. The caption reads, “Hezekiah was deeply troubled. He turned his face toward the wall, wept, and prayed to the Lord.” The king prays, “Please remember how I have walked before You faithfully, Lord.”
8. Isaiah is caught mid-step in the palace corridor, leaning on his staff and turning back with one hand raised, as though someone has just called him. The caption reads, “Before Isaiah had gone far, the Lord sent him another message.”
9. Isaiah is back at the bedside with both hands open, and the king has lifted himself up on one arm, smiling. The caption reads, “God had heard Hezekiah’s prayer. Isaiah hurried back to the king with God’s answer.” Isaiah says, “The Lord has heard your prayer and seen your tears. He will add fifteen years to your life!”
10. The king stands on the balcony again, well and crowned, both arms open to the sky. The caption reads, “God heard Hezekiah’s prayer and promised him fifteen more years of life.”
11. Back in the bedroom, the boy sits cross-legged on his bed with a Bible stories book open beside him, one hand out as he talks to Halo. The caption reads, “The boy thought about King Hezekiah and wondered if he could pray to God too.” The boy asks, “Halo, can I pray to God when I’m worried too?”
12. The boy presses his hands to his chest, beaming up at Halo. The caption reads, “Halo reminded the boy that he could always come to God in prayer.” Halo says, “Yes! You can talk to God whenever you’re worried, sad, or afraid. He wants you to come to Him in prayer.” The boy answers, “Then I’ll pray for Mum!”
13. The boy kneels on the rug beside his bed with his eyes closed and his hands folded, Halo glowing beside him. The caption reads, “The boy decided to follow King Hezekiah’s example and come to God in prayer.” He prays, “Dear Lord, please help Mum get better. Forgive me when I do wrong, and help me trust You. Amen.” Below, a My Decision panel reads “Like King Hezekiah, I shall come before the Lord with repentance and pray.” and a Memory Verse panel reads “The Lord is close to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit.” Psalm 34:18.

**Artwork.** Thirteen panels and both covers came from Drive’s “04 - Story Artwork”, all fifteen distinct by SHA-256. The nineteen text cards this chapter first shipped with are gone rather than reconciled, which is what happened to Beginner Chapters 5 to 13 and to Primary 1 to 3: the panels letter their own captions and speech, so a card that repeated them underneath would print the same sentence twice. Each card is now a picture with `alt` describing the scene and quoting what is lettered on it.

Panels 1, 9 and 11 are 941×1671, one pixel short of the canonical 941×1672. They were converted without distortion and nothing resized them; it is recorded here rather than fixed, since a one-pixel difference is invisible and the artwork is the teacher’s.

Panel 13 prints the Memory Verse as “Psalm 34:18” — singular — where p.66 prints “Psalms 34:18.”. The `reference` field keeps the book’s form, and the verse ladder’s stage 5 uses the artwork’s, for the reason set out there.
