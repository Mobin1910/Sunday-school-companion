## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy sits at his desk with his chin in his hands, looking worried. Above him the question reads, “What do you do when you are worried or have a problem?”, with three small pictures — a difficult test, a problem with a friend, and something that gets broken. Halo glows beside him and says, “You can tell Jesus about it! He always listens and cares.”
2. Halo says, “Someone in the Bible had a problem too.” A thought bubble above the boy shows Jesus and Mary at a wedding feast. The boy asks, “Really? What happened?”
3. The boy lies on his bed with a big storybook open in front of him. Halo says, “Let’s find out what happened!” and the picture above shows the wedding at Cana, with the words, “Jesus, his mother Mary, and his disciples were invited to a wedding in Cana.”
4. Jesus and his mother Mary walk with his disciples through a flowered archway. A sign reads, “Welcome to the Wedding in Cana.” The words above say, “Jesus, his mother Mary, and his disciples were invited to a wedding in Cana.”
5. A long table full of food and flowers, with musicians and guests all around. Jesus and Mary sit together among them. The words say, “The wedding was a happy celebration. Everyone was enjoying the feast!”
6. A servant holds an empty jar and another spreads his hands, both worried. Empty cups stand on the table. The words say, “But during the feast, the wine ran out.”
7. Mary stands behind a pillar, listening, while three servants talk beside the six stone jars. One says, “The wine has run out!” and another says, “What can we do now?” The words say, “Mary overheard the servants talking about the problem.”
8. Mary leans towards Jesus with her hand on her chest and says, “They have no wine.” Jesus listens. The words say, “Mary went to Jesus and told him about the problem.”
9. Mary turns to the servants by the stone jars and says, “If he commands you to do anything, do it.” The words say, “Mary told the servants to do whatever Jesus said.”
10. Jesus points towards the stone jars and says, “Fill the jars with water,” while the servants begin to pour. The words say, “Jesus told the servants what to do.”
11. The servants pour water from smaller jugs into the six big stone jars, and the water shines as it falls. The words say, “The servants filled the six stone jars with water.”
12. The water in the six stone jars has turned deep red. The servants and Mary look at it in astonishment. The words say, “The water became wine!”
13. A servant lifts a golden cup, delighted, and says, “It’s wine! Wonderful wine!” The words say, “The servants tasted the water, and it was wine!”
14. Jesus raises a cup among the smiling guests and one of them says, “This is amazing!” The words say, “Jesus did his first miracle at the wedding in Cana. He showed his glory, and his disciples believed in him.” The reference below reads John 2:11.
15. The boy is back at his desk at home, resting his chin on his hand. Halo glows beside him and asks, “Do you ever have problems that make you worried or sad?”
16. The boy looks up, easier now, and Halo says, “Jesus cares about you. You can tell Him everything.”
17. The boy kneels by his bed with his hands folded and his eyes closed, Halo glowing beside him. A card reads, “My decision: I will tell Jesus everything,” and beneath it, 1 Peter 5:7 — “Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you.”

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Core lesson: when we have a problem or are worried, we can tell Jesus, because he cares for us and can help us. The chapter's decision, and the line it lands on, is “I will tell Jesus everything.” Three acts, as Chapter 1: Connect — the modern child and Story Halo, a worry the child already knows; Discover — Cana itself, Story Halo absent; Reflect — back to the child, Story Halo returns. The panel sequence and every line of dialogue are pending approval and are deliberately not written down here: a numbered list of events sitting in this file would be read as a storyboard, and this chapter does not have one yet. Jesus and Mary are recurring characters — face, age, hair, skin, proportions and rendering language stay as Chapter 1 established them, and only clothing, pose and composition change.
