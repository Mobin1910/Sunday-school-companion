## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy in a backpack crouches on the rocks beside a shallow stream and looks at the rubbish caught in it — a crumpled can, a plastic bottle, dead leaves. Halo floats beside him. The boy asks, “What if we didn't have clean water?” and Halo answers, “That would be a big problem.”
2. The boy sits back on a rock with his chin on his hand, looking up. He asks, “Has this happened before?” Halo answers, “Yes. A long time ago, people in Jericho had a problem with their water too,” and a bright cloud opens behind them showing a walled city above a dry riverbed.
3. Elisha stands with his staff outside the walls of Jericho while a group of townspeople press towards him — one man with his hands open, a woman holding a clay jar. “Long ago, God's prophet Elisha was in the city of Jericho. The people of the city came to him.”
4. The man at the front spreads both hands as he speaks to Elisha, the others gathered close behind him. “As you know, this is a fine city, but the water is bad and nothing grows on the land.” Another voice adds, “Please help us.”
5. Elisha lifts one hand as he answers, the people watching him closely. “Elisha knew what to do.” He says, “Bring me a new bowl, and put salt in it.”
6. A man holds out a shallow clay bowl heaped with white salt, and Elisha reaches out to take it. “So, they brought him a new bowl with salt in it.”
7. Elisha walks down the stony path towards the water, the bowl of salt in one hand and his staff in the other, the people following behind. “With that bowl, the prophet went to a spring of water.”
8. Seen from behind, Elisha stands at the edge of a green, clouded pool holding the bowl, while the townspeople watch from the far bank. “The water in the spring was bad.”
9. Elisha tips the bowl and the salt falls in a bright stream into the pool. “Then Elisha threw the salt into the water.” He says, “In the name of the Lord, let this water be pure.”
10. The pool runs clear and blue over its stones. Elisha kneels smiling among the people, a girl cups the water in her hands, and Halo glows beside them. “And the water became pure.”
11. Back in his own street, the boy sits on a low wall with his chin on his hands, a water bottle beside him lettered “Clean Water Brighter Tomorrows” and a stack of books at his feet. Halo floats above him. “Water is a precious gift from God.”
12. The boy looks up at Halo beside a stone trough with water pouring into it, a small painted sign propped nearby reading “Clean Water Happier Tomorrow”. He asks, “So I should take care of water too?” Halo answers, “Yes. We have a responsibility to keep it clean.”
13. The boy reaches up and turns off a brass tap on the wall, grinning, with Halo beaming beside him and a board reading “Small Actions Keep Our World Beautiful”. He says, “I will keep the water clean.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 05 “Prophet Elisha Cleanses the Water”, p.28 — “Answer the Questions”*

**1. Who is the prophet mentioned in this lesson?**  
*What the child must take away:* Name Elisha as God's prophet, and know he is the one the people of Jericho came to.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Elisha. “Friends, when God's prophet Elisha was in the city of Jericho, people of the city came to him.” (p.28)

**2. What was problem that people of Jericho were facing?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know the trouble in one piece: the water was bad, so nothing grew on the land.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The water was bad and nothing grew. “As you know, this is a fine city, but the water is bad and nothing grows on land. Please help us.” (p.28)

**3. What did prophet Elisha do to purify water?**  
*What the child must take away:* Retrace what he did, in order: a new bowl, salt in it, thrown into the spring, in the name of the Lord.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* “Bring me a new bowl, and put salt in it.” … “the prophet went to a spring of water. Then he threw the salt into the water, saying, ‘In the name of the Lord, let this water be pure.’” (p.28)

**4. What are the properties of salt?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know what salt is like — that it dissolves away into water, keeps food from spoiling, and gives it taste.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Not stated in the lesson. See the note.

**5. For what purposes is salt used?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know what salt is for at home, and that this everyday thing is what God used at Jericho.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Not stated in the lesson. See the note.

**6. What happened to the water when salt was put in the spring?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know the outcome and that it lasted: the water became pure, and has been pure ever since.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* It became pure, and stayed so. “And, the water has been pure ever since.” (p.28)
