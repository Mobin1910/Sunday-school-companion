## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 12 “BABY MOSES”, p.57 — “TELL THE ANSWERS”*

**1. Moses belongs to ______ tribe (family).**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that his mother was a woman from the tribe of Levi.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Levi. “During this time, a woman from the tribe of Levi gave birth to a son.” (p.56)

**2. Moses was hidden for ______ months.**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that his mother hid him for three months.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Three. “When she saw what a fine baby he was, she hid him for three months.” (p.56)

**3. Moses was laid in a basket made of ______**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the basket was made of reeds and covered with tar.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Reeds. “she took a basket made of reeds and covered it with tar to make it watertight.” (p.56)

**4. Who watched from afar to see what would happen to Moses?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the baby’s sister stood some distance away and watched.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* His sister. “The baby’s sister stood some distance away to see what would happen to him.” (p.56)

**5. Who came to bath in the river?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the Pharoah’s daughter came down to the river to bath.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The Pharoah’s daughter. “Then, the Pharoah’s daughter came down to the river to bath. And, her servants walked along the riverbank.” (p.56)

**6. Who named Moses?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that the Pharoah’s daughter named him.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The Pharoah’s daughter. “Later, when the child was old enough, she took him to the Pharoah’s daughter. She said, ‘I pulled him out of the water’ and named him Moses.” (p.56) The chapter’s memory verse on p.58 settles it: “She named him Moses, saying, ‘I drew him out of the water’.”

**7. What is the reason for naming him Moses?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that she said, “I pulled him out of the water”.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* The water. “She said, ‘I pulled him out of the water’ and named him Moses.” (p.56) The memory verse has it as “I drew him out of the water”.

**8. Who all helped to save baby Moses?**  
*What the child must take away:* Know that his mother, his sister, the princess and her servant woman each had a part.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Four of them, and the book asks for all four. His mother hid him and made the basket; his sister watched and spoke up; the servant woman fetched the basket out of the reeds; and the Pharoah’s daughter felt sorry for him, paid his own mother to nurse him, and took him as her own. (p.56)

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the four curriculum photographs in Drive’s “01 - Curriculum Source” for Beginner / Chapter 12 — the opener (p.55), the lesson (p.56), the eight questions with the river drawing (p.57) and the close (p.58). All four are around 2 to 3 MB and were read by eye at full size.

The chapter’s “06 - Memory Verse” folder is empty, which is right for this class: Beginner prints its memory verse on the chapter’s own closing page, and Exodus 2:10 (b) was read from p.58.

**The artwork is in.** Fifteen files from “04 - Story Artwork” — a cover, a landscape cover and panels 1 to 13 — PNG to WebP at native size, no resize and no crop. Every one is 941×1672 or its transpose, and all fifteen are distinct.

**The twenty-three text cards are gone.** Every panel is lettered, captions and speech bubbles both, so the app writing the same sentence underneath would say it twice and the caption layout would crop the art to make room. The story is thirteen picture-only cards, one per panel, each with alt text that describes the scene and quotes what is written in it — the same thing Chapters 5 to 11 did when their artwork arrived.

**The artwork brought a present-day frame with it.** Panels 1, 2, 12 and 13 are a boy, a younger girl and Halo in a classroom and a school yard; the Egypt story runs from panel 3 to panel 11 between them. The book has no such frame on its pages, but this class’s artwork has had one since Chapter 5.

**Two things for the artist**, both recorded on the panels themselves: panel 3 puts “Destroy the Hebrew baby boys!” in Pharaoh’s mouth, which is stronger than the book’s own prose and stronger than the text card it replaces; and panel 12 names the sister Miriam, which neither the lesson nor Exodus 2 does.

**All four games now carry a shelf picture.** A shelf card is read before the game is played, so it may letter a question or a trouble but never an answer a child will be marked on. That rules out most of this chapter: panels 4, 5 and 6 letter the three blanks, panel 7 letters who came to bathe, panel 11 letters both the naming and the reason, and panels 9 and 10 letter the nursing. What is left — the framing panels and panels 3 and 8 — is what the games carry; see each game’s own note.

**There is no prayer in this chapter, so none is written here.** Both prayer slots are stage directions rather than prayers: p.55 says “(Either teacher or one of the children should pray)” and p.58 says “(One of the children should pray)”. Every other chapter in this class prints words. Writing some would be putting them in the Samajam’s mouth, so the `prayer` block is left out altogether and this note is the record of why.

**Eight questions and four games.** Four is this band’s ceiling, so the games carry two or three interactions each and name every question they answer. The grouping follows the lesson’s own joints: the hiding and the basket, the people at the river, the naming, and — last, because it is the point — everyone who had a hand in it.

**The king’s order is named, not described.** The book opens “The Pharoah (King) issued an order to destroy the male children of the Hebrews”. The story card says the king gave a cruel order against their baby boys, which keeps the danger the whole chapter depends on without putting the killing of babies in front of a six-year-old. Everything after it is the book’s own wording.

“Pharoah” is the book’s spelling throughout and is kept.
