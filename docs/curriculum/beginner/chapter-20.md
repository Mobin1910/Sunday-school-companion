## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A classroom. A boy in a yellow shirt stands with his head lowered while a boy in a teal shirt points at him and shouts, “You’re no good at this! I don’t want you on my team!” Other children look up from their desks. The caption reads, “A classmate spoke unkindly to the boy. His words hurt.”
2. The boy in yellow sits at his desk with his chin on his hand, Halo glowing beside him under a “Be Kind” poster. Halo asks, “That sounded hurtful. How did it make you feel?” and the boy answers, “Sad… I didn’t like it.” The caption reads, “The boy begins to recognise the impact of his words.”
3. The boy looks up and asks, “Why are some people unkind to others?” Halo answers, “That’s a thoughtful question. Let’s meet someone who stayed faithful to Jesus when people were unkind to him.” A glowing cloud opens above them with a man speaking to a crowd inside it. The caption reads, “Halo suggests learning from Stephen’s story.”
4. The vision fills the room: a man in a white robe and green cloak speaks with both hands open to a crowd outside a city wall. Halo says, “This is Stephen. He loved Jesus and shared about Him, but some people were very unkind to him.” and the boy asks, “Oh… What happened to him?” The caption reads, “Halo shows the boy Stephen, who stayed faithful to Jesus even when people were unkind.”
5. Stephen kneels on the stony ground with his hands folded, men all around him with stones raised. He prays, “Lord Jesus, forgive them, because they don’t know what they are doing.” The caption reads, “Even when people threw stones at him, Stephen prayed for them.”
6. Stephen has fallen onto his hands among the stones, one arm stretched out, the men still throwing. He prays, “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.” The caption reads, “As the people kept throwing stones, Stephen prayed to the Lord.”
7. Stephen lifts one hand to a bright opening in the sky where Jesus stands in the light, his own face turned up and shining. He says, “Look, I see the heavens open and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God.” The caption reads, “As Stephen prayed, he saw Jesus standing in heaven.”
8. Stephen kneels with his head bowed and his hands folded, still and quiet, the men behind him blurred. The caption reads, “Stephen prayed, ‘Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.’ Then he died. He was the first martyr of the Church.”
9. Back in the classroom, the boy rests his chin on his hand with a thought cloud above him holding the whole scene — Stephen among the stones and Jesus in the light. The caption reads, “The boy thought about Stephen. Even when people were mean to him, he chose to pray and forgive.”
10. The boy is smiling up at Halo, who asks, “What do you think Stephen teaches us?” The boy answers, “That I should pray for people, even when they are unkind.” A green banner at the foot of the picture reads, “I will pray for those who are unkind to me.”

## 3. The lesson's own questions

*Beginners Mar Thoma Vol-1, Chapter 20 “Stephen”, p.92 — “Tell the Answers”*

**1. What did the disciples do after the crucifixion of Jesus?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that the disciples went to many places and preached about Jesus.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* They went to many places and preached about Jesus. “After the crucifixion of Jesus, the disciples went to many places. Everywhere they preached about Jesus.” (p.92)

**2. What are the qualities that we see in Stephen?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Stephen had good faith, knowledge and testimony.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Good faith, knowledge and testimony — and he did miracles by the power of the Holy Spirit. “He had good faith, knowledge and testimony. He performed many miracles among the people by the power of the Holy Spirit.” (p.92)

**3. How did Stephen die?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that the people stoned Stephen to death.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He was stoned to death. “They told the people to stone him to death.” (p.92)

**4. What was the main reason for stoning Stephen?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Stephen preached about Jesus, and that this is what angered them.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* He preached about Jesus. “…complained to the high priest that Stephen was preaching against them… There also he preached about Jesus. When they heard that, their anger increased.” (p.92)

**5. Who was the first martyr of the church?**  
*What the child must take away:* Remember that Stephen was the first martyr of the Church.  
*Answer, quoted from the lesson:* Stephen. “Thus Stephan became the first martyr of the Church.” (p.92, which spells the name “Stephan” in this sentence)

## 5. The games this chapter already has

Artwork is reused as game cards, so this matters while you draw:

- a card may letter a **question**, never an **answer** a child is marked on;
- the shelf crops each card to a **centre square** and prints the game's title across the bottom of it, so any lettering must survive that crop whole and sit clear of the title;
- the whole shelf is read before any game is played, so a picture that letters **any** game's answer is barred from **every** card.

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Built from the curriculum photographs the teacher added for Chapter 20: three pages, p.91 (title, prayer, Bible portion), p.92 (the lesson and “Tell the Answers”) and p.93 (My Decision, the song, the Memory Verse and the Concluding Prayer). **The chapter is complete** — nothing missing, as in Chapters 16 and 19. The three photographs are inside “01 - Curriculum Source” where they belong, unlike Chapter 19’s, which were loose in the chapter folder.

Four games, five of five questions covered, two of four choose-one-of-N — the most the Beginner band allows.

**The lesson spells the name three ways in two sentences.** p.92’s second paragraph opens “Stephan looked up to heaven…”, then “Stephen writhed in pain and died”, then “Thus Stephan became the first martyr of the Church.” The title, the first paragraph and the questions all print **Stephen**, which is what this chapter uses throughout and what the artwork letters. The two “Stephan”s look like a typesetting slip rather than a second name, but they are the teacher’s page and nothing here was changed on her behalf — it is recorded so she can decide.

**The Memory Verse reference is printed “St. Mathew 5:44”, with one t.** See the verse note: the app prints St. Matthew, as every other chapter in this app already does.

**Artwork.** Ten panels and both covers came from Drive’s “04 - Story Artwork”, all twelve distinct by SHA-256, the folder enumerated by exact filename rather than by paging — a listing pages five at a time here and repeats rows across page boundaries, which is how a landscape cover nearly went missing from this chapter. Every story card is a picture with `alt` describing the scene and quoting what is lettered on it, which is where Beginner Chapters 5 to 19 and Primary 1 to 3 now stand.

The frame story is a classroom: a boy is told “You’re no good at this!” by a classmate, Halo asks him how that felt, and Stephen’s story is what Halo brings him. It lands on the Memory Verse rather than on the lesson — the boy’s own answer in panel 10 is “That I should pray for people, even when they are unkind.”

**Two things in the artwork are worth the teacher’s eye.** First, the order: panel 6 has Stephen pray “Lord Jesus, receive my spirit”, panel 7 then has him see heaven open, and panel 8’s caption gives the same prayer again before he dies. In Acts 7 the vision comes first and the prayer once. The panels are carried in the order they are numbered, because that order is the artist’s and it reads gently — the light before the end — but the prayer does appear twice. Second, panel 2’s caption reads “The boy begins to recognise the impact of his words”, and the boy in the picture is the one who was spoken to, not the one who spoke; his line is “Sad… I didn’t like it.” The caption appears to belong to the other boy.
