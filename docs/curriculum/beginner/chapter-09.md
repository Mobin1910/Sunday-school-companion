## 1. The story, beat by beat

*These describe panels that already exist — each says what is drawn and quotes what is lettered on it.*

1. A boy sits cross-legged on a patterned rug with a big book open in front of him, the evening sun through the window behind. A thought cloud above shows a man in ragged clothes sitting against a stone wall with a staff. Halo glows beside him. The caption reads, “Sometimes we wonder why difficult things happen to people.” The boy asks, “Why do some people have to suffer, Halo?” and Halo answers, “Let’s discover what Jesus teaches us about that.”
2. The book open on the rug and glowing, and the scene inside it fills the page: Jesus in a red mantle and His disciples walking a stony road up towards the walls and gate of Jerusalem. The boy watches over the top of the page. The caption reads, “Long ago, Jesus and His disciples were travelling to Jerusalem.” Halo says, “Come, let’s see what happened,” and the boy asks, “What happened to the man?”
3. A man in worn clothes sits against a sunlit wall with a wooden staff and a clay bowl beside him, his face turned up and his eyes unfocused. Jesus comes along the street with His disciples, one of them pointing. The caption reads, “On the way, Jesus and His disciples met a man who had been blind since birth.” A disciple says, “Look, Teacher. There is a blind man.”
4. The disciples gathered close around Jesus, one with his hand out, asking. The blind man sits on the steps behind them with his staff. The caption reads, “The disciples wondered why the man had been born blind.” The disciple asks, “Teacher, why was this man born without sight?”
5. Jesus answering, both hands open, the disciples listening around Him and the blind man still on the steps behind. The caption reads, “Jesus told them that the man’s blindness was not because he or his parents had sinned.” Jesus says, “No one has sinned. But now you will know how God gives him vision.”
6. Jesus kneeling on the ground, both hands in the dust, making mud. The man sits beside Him holding his staff, head turned towards the sound; three disciples watch from behind. The caption reads, “Then Jesus spat on the ground. He made mud with His hands.” The man asks, “What are You doing, Lord?”
7. Jesus with both hands at the man’s face, laying the mud across his eyes. The disciples stand behind. The caption reads, “Jesus rubbed the mud on both of the man’s eyes.” Jesus says, “Now go and wash your face in the Pool of Siloam,” and the man answers, “Yes, Lord. I will go.”
8. Seen from behind: the man walks down a stone alley with his staff, one hand on the wall, towards a wide green pool with steps down into it and people sitting at its edge. The caption reads, “The man went to the Pool of Siloam and washed his face.” He thinks, “I will trust Jesus.”
9. At the water’s edge, the man kneeling with water running off his face and hands at his open eyes, laughing. People on the steps behind point at him. The caption reads, “When he washed the mud away, he could see!” He cries, “I can see! I can see!” and a woman behind says, “He can see!”
10. The man kneeling in front of Jesus with his hands held in both of Jesus’, looking up at Him; the disciples smiling around them. The caption reads, “The man came back to Jesus and thanked Him. He knelt down before Jesus.” The man says, “Thank You, Jesus!”
11. Jesus standing among the seated disciples with His hands open, the healed man sitting beside them with his hand raised. The caption reads, “Jesus then asked His disciples if they were convinced that God can heal anyone.” Jesus asks, “Are you now convinced that God can heal anyone?”
12. Back in the room, the boy sitting with the open Bible in his lap, chin on his hands, looking up at Halo; a cross on the bookshelf behind and a star-printed curtain at the window. The caption reads, “Sometimes we may not understand why difficult things happen.” The boy asks, “So even when I don’t understand, I can trust God?” and Halo answers, “Yes. God can bring light into our darkness.”
13. The boy sitting with his eyes closed and a wide smile, hugging a brown Bible with a gold cross to his chest; Halo beside him with its eyes closed too. The caption reads, “When I don’t understand everything, I can still trust God.” The boy says, “I will trust God and follow His light.”

## 7. What the build recorded about this chapter

Beginner, Chapter 9, from the Mar Thoma Sunday School Samajam Beginners Vol-1, pages 43–46. The man born blind, told as a chapter about what to do with a question you cannot answer.

**The first chapter in this library built against its curriculum and its artwork at the same time.** Every one before it got its games written from text and then rebuilt when the art arrived; this one was planned once, with both in hand, which is why no game here had to be re-cut.

Memory verse and five-stage practice from the Memory Verse agent; see the verse note.

Games from the Game Builder agent (`npm run agent:games -- --class beginner --chapter 09 --plan`). Its first run mis-read four of the seven questions, which is the worst run the classifier has had and the most it has been taught in one chapter: asking is speech (“What did the disciples ask Jesus when they saw the blind man?” went to `scene`, because the perception rule reads the word “saw”); the noun “answer” is speech too (“What was Jesus’ answer to them?” reached no rule at all); a pool is a place (“In which pool did Jesus say to go and wash?” went to `saying`, because it contains “did Jesus say”); and a definition is none of those (“What is the meaning of the word ‘Siloam’?” had no category). The second run planned what ships.

One mechanic was changed by hand, and the reason is in that game’s note.
